# 01. Chart of Accounts

## Overview

The Chart of Accounts (COA) is the structured list of accounts used to classify and record an organization's financial transactions.

It provides the foundation for journal entries, general ledger processing, trial balance preparation, and financial reporting.

## Account Categories

### Assets

Assets represent resources controlled by the business.

Examples:

- Cash & Bank
- Accounts Receivable
- Inventory
- Fixed Assets

### Liabilities

Liabilities represent obligations owed by the business.

Examples:

- Accounts Payable
- Accrued Expenses

### Equity

Equity represents the owners' residual interest in the business.

Example:

- Equity

### Revenue

Revenue represents income generated from business activities.

Example:

- Sales Revenue

### Expenses

Expenses represent costs incurred in generating revenue.

Examples:

- Cost of Goods Sold
- Operating Expenses
- Depreciation Expense

## Normal Balance

The normal balance indicates whether an account normally carries a debit or credit balance.

| Account Category | Normal Balance |
|---|---|
| Assets | Debit |
| Expenses | Debit |
| Liabilities | Credit |
| Equity | Credit |
| Revenue | Credit |

## R2R Importance

A properly maintained Chart of Accounts helps ensure:

- Consistent transaction classification
- Accurate journal entries
- Reliable General Ledger reporting
- Proper financial statement classification
- Easier account reconciliation
- Stronger financial controls

## Controls

Key COA controls include:

- Unique account codes
- Standardised account names
- Correct account classification
- Defined normal balances
- Controlled creation of new accounts
- Periodic review of inactive accounts

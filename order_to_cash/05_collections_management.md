# 05. Collections Management

## Overview

Collections Management is the process of monitoring outstanding customer receivables and following up on overdue balances to support timely cash recovery.

It is a key part of the Order-to-Cash (O2C) lifecycle and directly connects Accounts Receivable ageing with customer payment activity.

## Key Collections Activities

### 1. Review Outstanding Receivables

Outstanding customer balances are reviewed regularly to identify invoices requiring collection action.

Key information includes:

- Customer
- Invoice number
- Invoice date
- Due date
- Outstanding amount
- Days overdue
- Ageing bucket
- Collection status

### 2. Prioritise Accounts

Collection activity can be prioritised based on:

- Amount outstanding
- Days overdue
- Customer payment history
- Credit exposure
- Dispute status
- Payment commitment

Higher-value and significantly overdue balances generally require closer monitoring.

### 3. Customer Follow-up

Collection follow-ups may include:

- Email reminders
- Payment-status enquiries
- Statement sharing
- Payment confirmation requests
- Escalation to account management
- Dispute resolution coordination

### 4. Payment Commitment Tracking

When a customer confirms an expected payment date, the commitment should be recorded and monitored.

5. Dispute Management

Some overdue invoices may be related to disputes rather than unwillingness to pay.

Common billing disputes include:

Incorrect pricing
Incorrect quantity
Missing purchase order
Duplicate invoice
Service-related issue
Tax discrepancy

Disputed invoices should be coordinated with the relevant internal teams for resolution.

Collections KPIs
Collection Rate
Collection Rate = Total Collections / Total Invoiced × 100
Outstanding Receivables

Measures the total unpaid customer balance.

Overdue Receivables

Measures the portion of Accounts Receivable that has passed its due date.

Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)

DSO measures the average number of days required to collect customer receivables.

DSO = Average Accounts Receivable / Credit Sales × Number of Days
Accounting Impact

Successful collection reduces Accounts Receivable.

Typical accounting entry:

Bank / Cash              Dr
       Accounts Receivable      Cr
